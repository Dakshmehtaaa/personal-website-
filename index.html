Flagship sustainability work

SustainSwap is a student-to-student second-hand marketplace built around one promise: the first hassle-free second hand solution. Instead of leaving students to photograph, list, haggle and ship items themselves, the model routes everything through the university. Students log in with their university access, register goods for sale and send them to SustainSwap, which verifies, values and refurbishes each item before it is published. Buyers order online and either collect from a campus pick-up point or receive the item by eco-friendly delivery.

The project covers the full commercial build rather than just the idea. Segmentation runs on socio-professional status, environmental awareness and sustainable shopping behaviour; targeting focuses on budget-conscious settlers, eco-conscious students and international students. Positioning is worked through the complete marketing mix — resale economics that repay the initial investment several times over, subscription and upfront-with-partial-refund pricing to give students financial flexibility, a central warehouse with campus pick-up boxes for logistics, and student ambassadors, pop-up shops and social campaigns timed to the start of each semester for promotion.

  • Focus Circular economy · reuse · student affordability
  • Deliverables Investor pitch deck, marketing poster, product demo

FREITAG makes bags out of used truck tarpaulins, seatbelts and bicycle inner tubes — one of the few businesses where the waste stream is the raw material. That makes it an unusually honest test case for circular economy thinking, because the circularity is not an add-on to the product, it is the product.

This analysis maps the value chain end to end: sourcing and cleaning used tarpaulins, cutting and design, manufacture, distribution, and the repair and resale loop that keeps bags in use. The question running through it is where circularity actually creates margin and defensibility rather than just marketing language. The work was built first as a collaborative FigJam whiteboard — mapping material flows and stakeholders visually — and then presented as a deck.

  • Focus Upcycling · material flows · circular margin
  • Format Presentation plus a live FigJam whiteboard

An investment analysis of the BNP Paribas Easy ECPI Circular Economy Leaders UCITS ETF, presented as a pitch to a prospective investor. The fund tracks companies that ECPI identifies as leaders in circular economy practice, which makes it a useful place to ask the question that matters most in green finance: does the label survive contact with the actual holdings?

The analysis works through the screening methodology behind the index, what the fund holds as a result, and how it has performed — then weighs it on financial and sustainability grounds together rather than treating them as separate scorecards. It sits at the intersection I care about most: capital allocation as a sustainability lever, not just a reporting exercise.

  • Focus Green finance · ESG screening · fund analysis
  • Subject BNP Paribas Easy ECPI Circular Economy Leaders UCITS ETF

International trade is one of the hardest emissions problems there is: the goods move because the economics say they should, and the emissions follow the ships, the ports and the length of the supply chain. This project asks where innovation can genuinely cut that footprint rather than simply move it somewhere less visible.

It looks across the trade system — freight modes and routing, port and customs operations, and how supply chains are designed in the first place — to identify the levers that actually shift emissions at scale. It is the same problem space I went on to work in at CEVA Logistics, inside the CSR team of a global freight operator.

  • Focus Freight emissions · supply chain design · trade policy

Corporate strategy & analysis

A scenario-planning exercise on SHEIN's future in Europe. No consumer sector is under a sharper sustainability squeeze than ultra-fast fashion — textile waste rules, incoming due-diligence regulation, and a consumer conversation that has turned sceptical — and none has grown faster in spite of it.

Rather than committing to a single forecast, the deck builds out alternative futures for the European market and tests which strategic choices hold up across all of them. That is the useful discipline in scenario planning: it forces a strategy to be judged on robustness rather than on how good the base case looks.

  • Focus Scenario planning · market entry · regulatory risk
  • Format Group project, 12-page deck

Sustainability conversations default to carbon, but the social pillar is where corporate exposure is often most acute and least measured. This study examines child labour in Bolivia — the economic conditions that sustain it, the policy and legal response, and the gap between the two.

The relevance to corporate practice is direct: human rights due diligence across supply chains is moving from voluntary reporting to legal obligation, and understanding why child labour persists in a given economy is the difference between a policy that works and a clause in a supplier contract that nobody enforces.

Back Market is Europe's largest marketplace for refurbished electronics, and its entire environmental case rests on something unglamorous: whether customers trust that a refurbished device will still be supported after they buy it. Extending a phone's life by a few years is one of the highest-leverage things a consumer can do for their footprint, but only if the after-sales experience holds.

This workbook analyses post-sale service quality across that layer — structuring the raw service data, building the quality checks, and reading what the results say about where the experience breaks down. It is the quiet operational work that decides whether a circular business model actually keeps devices in circulation.

  • Focus After-sales quality · refurbished electronics · reuse

Intel Data Center Manager is software for monitoring power, thermals and utilisation across a data centre estate. This is a strategic SWOT of the product: its strengths and weaknesses as a platform, the competitive pressure around it, and where the opportunity and threat actually sit.

The sustainability angle is not incidental. Data centre electricity consumption is both a major operating cost and a fast-growing share of ICT emissions, which means energy efficiency has quietly become a commercial argument rather than a compliance one — and that reframing is what makes the category interesting.

Strategies rarely fail on the whiteboard; they fail in execution. This project takes implementation seriously as its own phase of the strategic management process — the point where ideas have to convert into tangible actions and outcomes — and sets out six factors that determine whether that conversion works: clear communication, careful planning, resources, evaluation, flexibility and collaboration.

Those six are then tested against real companies. Samsung serves as the cautionary case, where a lack of testing, poor communication and a failure to learn from previous mistakes undermined execution. Amazon is the counterweight — investment in technology innovation, a diversified patent portfolio, the "Alexa Everywhere" rollout, AWS as the engine of operating income, and a pattern of partnerships and logistics integration that turned strategy into infrastructure.

  • Focus Execution · change management · case analysis
  • Team Salena Bacar, Clementine Damide, Junjie Liao, William Koncevich, Daksh Mehta, Luana Saintot
