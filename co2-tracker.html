The basics

Scope 1, 2 and 3 — in plain language

The GHG Protocol splits a company's emissions into three buckets. The split isn't about how big the emissions are — it's about who controls the source.

Scope 1

What you burn

Direct emissions from sources you own or control: fuels and refrigerant leakage.

Typical lines: natural gas, heating oil, LPG, company-owned vehicles, refrigerant top-ups.

Scope 2

What you buy as energy

Indirect emissions from purchased energy. This calculator uses location-based grid electricity.

Typical lines: purchased electricity, district heating and cooling.

Scope 3

Everything else in your value chain

Other indirect emissions across 15 value-chain categories. This tool estimates selected activities only.

Typical lines: business travel, commuting, waste, water, purchased goods and services.

Coverage is partial: common fuels and refrigerants, purchased electricity and heat, and selected Scope 3 activities. Process emissions, most value-chain categories and market-based Scope 2 are not calculated.

    Method

    Method & sources

    Activity data × emission factor = kgCO₂e. Factors and coverage are visible below.

    Accounting basis

    Uses the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard scope definitions and selected Scope 3 calculation methods. A complete compliant inventory requires more boundaries, categories and disclosures.

    Scope 2

    Location-based electricity only. Where required, a separate market-based result must follow the Scope 2 Guidance quality criteria. This tool does not provide that result.

    Emission factors

    Built-in factors are verified against DESNZ 2026. Most are UK-specific; hotel stays use France. Grid and spend inputs require a published source. Flight factors exclude non-CO₂ radiative forcing.

    Where GRI fits

    GRI guides sustainability disclosure. It is not an emission-factor database, and this calculator is not a GRI report or a certified inventory.
