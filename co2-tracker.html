The basics

Scope 1, 2 and 3 — in plain language

The GHG Protocol splits a company's emissions into three buckets. The split isn't about how big the emissions are — it's about who controls the source.

Scope 1

What you burn

Direct emissions from sources you own or control: the gas boiler in your building, the diesel in your own vans, refrigerant leaking out of your air conditioning. If it comes out of a chimney or a tailpipe you're responsible for, it's Scope 1.

Typical lines: natural gas, heating oil, LPG, company-owned vehicles, refrigerant top-ups.

Scope 2

What you buy as energy

Indirect emissions from the electricity, heat, steam or cooling you purchase. You don't burn anything on site — someone else does, on your behalf. Which is why the number depends enormously on your country's grid: the same kilowatt-hour is roughly twelve times more carbon-intensive in Poland than in France.

Typical lines: purchased electricity, district heating and cooling.

Scope 3

Everything else in your value chain

The fifteen categories upstream and downstream of you — purchased goods and services, business travel, commuting, waste, transport, use of sold products. For most service companies this is 70–90% of the total, and it's the hardest to measure, which is exactly why screening estimates are useful before you invest in precision.

Typical lines: business travel, commuting, waste, water, purchased goods and services.

This tool covers Scope 1 and Scope 2 in full, and the Scope 3 categories most companies can estimate from data they already hold: purchased goods and services (1), fuel- and energy-related activities (3), waste (5), business travel (6) and commuting (7). The remaining categories need supplier-specific or product-level data and are out of scope for a screening tool.

      Method

      How this works, and what it isn't

      Every number above is activity data multiplied by a published emission factor. There is no model, no black box, and nothing you can't check.

      The arithmetic

      Emissions = activity data × emission factor. 10,000 kWh of gas × 0.18231 kgCO₂e/kWh = 1,823 kgCO₂e. That's the whole calculation, repeated per line and summed by scope. Results are shown in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent, which folds methane, nitrous oxide and refrigerants into a single comparable unit using their 100-year global warming potentials.

      Location-based Scope 2

      Electricity here uses the average carbon intensity of the grid you sit on. If you buy a green tariff or hold guarantees of origin, the GHG Protocol also wants a market-based figure, which can be far lower — this tool doesn't calculate that. Report both if you disclose formally.

      What it is not

      Not an audit-grade inventory, and not a substitute for one. Screening tools like this are for finding the 20% of lines that drive 80% of the footprint, so you know where to spend real measurement effort. For CSRD, CDP or SBTi submissions you need organisational boundaries, base-year rules, verified activity data and the factor set your assurance provider accepts.

      Where the factors come from

      Fuels, transport, waste and water use the UK Government's greenhouse gas conversion factors, which are free, well documented and widely used outside the UK too. Refrigerants use IPCC global warming potentials. Grid intensities are rounded national averages. Spend-based factors are environmentally-extended input-output screening ranges — deliberately blunt.
